G7e Electric Torpedo

Historical 3D reconstruction of the G7e electric torpedo using parametric modeling in Autodesk Fusion 360. Focused on mechanical accuracy, surface continuity, and historical fidelity.

Project Overview

The G7e was a historically significant electric torpedo used during World War II, renowned for its reduced wake signature and stealth operation compared to compressed-air counterparts. Powered by lead-acid batteries, it represented a major advancement in underwater weapon technology.

This personal CAD modeling project involved recreating the external geometry and structural layout of the G7e torpedo in Autodesk Fusion 360. The design process prioritized proportional accuracy, axial symmetry, and mechanical realism based on publicly available historical references and technical documentation.

Technical Approach

The modeling process was structured around parametric design principles, allowing for iterative refinement of dimensions and features. Key technical aspects included:

  • Parametric sketching of the cylindrical hull profile with historical dimension constraints
  • Complex multi-body assembly of warhead, battery section, motor housing, and propeller
  • Surface continuity analysis for hydrodynamic nose cone and tail cone transitions
  • Detailed propeller geometry with proper blade pitch and hub alignment
  • Reference-based proportion scaling for mechanical realism

Design Challenges

Reconstructing a historical weapon system from limited public-domain references presented several unique challenges:

  • Reference Scarcity: Limited high-resolution technical drawings required cross-referencing multiple sources
  • Proportional Accuracy: Balancing historical accuracy with practical modeling constraints
  • Surface Quality: Achieving Class-A surface continuity on compound curvature sections
  • Assembly Complexity: Managing inter-part references in a long, slender cylindrical form factor

Future Improvements

I am continuously refining this model with planned enhancements:

  • FEA simulation for hydrodynamic flow analysis

  • 3D printing feasibility study for scaled physical prototype
